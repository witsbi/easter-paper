**Status:** Reconciliation record. Not a new investigation. Does not change the frozen four-property conjunction stated in `paper/manuscript.md` §9.4, and does not select a single "winning" architecture as a manuscript claim — see the explicit conclusion below.

## 1. Pax's independent pass (as relayed)

Pax conducted an independent closest-architecture evaluation, scoring directly against EASTER's six named primitives (Evidence, Authority, State, Transition, Exception, Receipt) rather than Clawde's eight-property decomposition. Pax's verdict: Hyperledger Fabric is the single closest architecture — covering 2 primitives (durable exception records excluded from state; immutable history), partially covering 3 (Evidence via endorsements/private-data hashes; Authority via endorsement-policy gating inside the commit path; an outcome/Receipt-adjacent finding via the per-transaction valid/invalid indicator), and lacking 1 (Fabric designates a canonical world state, which EASTER refuses). Pax additionally evaluated Ethereum as a runner-up and found it "loses cleanly": Ethereum names its mechanism "transaction receipts" but keeps a canonical world-state trie and has no evidence object distinct from transitions. Pax's stated conclusion: the kill condition (some single system covering the whole bundle) was not triggered, and the systems that do receipts/exception-recording all designate canonical state while the systems that avoid canonical state (Pax named Certificate Transparency, Git, CRDTs) do no authority-gating, receipts, or exception recording at all — i.e., the conjunction occupies a real, structurally-explained gap, not an accidental one.

## 2. Facts vs. decomposition/weighting

Comparing Pax's pass against Clawde's two evaluation files, no underlying primary-source fact is contradicted between the two independent passes. Both find: Fabric's world-state view is mutable/current while its transaction/block history is immutable; Fabric's canonicality is enforced per-channel; Fabric's Authority runs through MSP+CRL+endorsement policy; Fabric's outcome-recording is conditional on reaching the ordering service. The disagreement is in **decomposition** (Pax scored directly against the six named primitives, folding canonicality in as a separate pass/fail gate rather than as one of several co-equal properties; Clawde split canonicality out as one of eight explicit co-equal sub-properties) and in **weighting** (an equal-weight read across Clawde's eight sub-properties nudges the "closest single architecture" question toward Corda; a weighting that treats Authority-with-revocation and uniform-Receipt as more central to EASTER's own stated identity than canonicality-abstention taken alone favors Fabric, which is where Pax's independent pass also lands). Two independent passes not contradicting each other at the evidence level, only at the aggregation level, was read as increasing rather than decreasing confidence in the underlying facts.

## 3. Point-by-point reconciliation

**3.1 — Fabric vs. Corda as "closest": is there a principled basis to name one, or should the paper refuse to rank?**
Conclusion: there is a principled basis to name one, provided the weighting basis is stated explicitly rather than presented as a single neutral scoring. Under equal-weighting across Clawde's eight sub-properties, Corda edges Fabric narrowly (fewer NOT-COVERED/demonstrated-absent findings). Under a weighting that treats Authority-with-revocation and uniform-Receipt as more central to EASTER's own identity than canonicality-abstention alone (canonicality-abstention being necessary but not sufficient — Git and CRDTs have it too, combined with no authority-gating at all), Fabric wins clearly, and this is where Pax's independent pass, using a different decomposition, also converges. Recommendation carried into this record: name Fabric as closest with the weighting stated as an explicit premise, and name Corda as the strongest alternative specifically on the axes where it wins (canonicality, evidence-separation, transition-explicitness). Refusing to rank at all was considered and rejected as a weaker, evasive option given two independent analyses converge once the weighting premise is made explicit.

**3.2 — Fabric's immutable history vs. mutable canonical world state, mapped precisely to EASTER State/Transition.**
Fabric's transaction/block log (immutable, MVCC-versioned, hash-chained) maps onto EASTER's **Transition** primitive (property 4 in Clawde's evaluation, COVERED). EASTER's **State** primitive specifically requires "immutable admitted work-state payloads... from which continuation may branch" — a directly-addressable, individually-retrievable, standalone immutable object. Fabric has no documented analogue to this: its world state is a mutable, in-place-updated current view (property 3, NOT COVERED for the "world state" half per Fabric's own documentation, which states the world state "can change frequently"), and any past state must be reconstructed by replaying the log rather than being already materialized as a first-class fetchable object. Sharpened reading carried into this record: Fabric Transition — COVERED; Fabric State as EASTER specifically defines it — NOT COVERED, not merely split. This is noted as a point that, if anything, favors EASTER's claim (a real thing Fabric does not do), not a point against it — adversarial review does not mean only reporting anti-EASTER findings.

**3.3 — Does Fabric's pre-ordering failure boundary materially weaken its Receipt comparison?**
Yes, materially. Proposal/endorsement failures — a large share of real client-facing error cases — never reach the ledger at all (Fabric's own documentation: "No updates are made to the ledger at this point"). EASTER's Receipt claim requires uniformity across accepted, rejected, **and failed** operations symmetrically; Fabric's uniformity (via `TxValidationCode`) is conditional on reaching the ordering service. This is a real gap in Fabric's candidacy, not a rounding error, and is preserved as Property 6 = PARTIAL (not COVERED) in `clawde-hyperledger-fabric-evaluation-2026-09-30.md`.

**3.4 — Does Corda's weaker/revocation-separated Authority materially weaken its candidacy?**
Yes. Corda's authority story splits across two non-communicating layers: per-transaction signature verification (governs who must sign a specific transaction, not whether an identity is currently allowed to act at all) and network-layer CRL revocation checked only at TLS handshake, explicitly documented as override-able via locally injecting signed `NodeInfo` files. Neither performs EASTER's specific claim of an in-path, kernel-internal, hard-rejecting authority check at the moment of admission. Because Authority is one of EASTER's six named primitives and is under independent adversarial scrutiny already (the confused-deputy exposure identified in the earlier prior-art pass, `paper/reviews/clawde-draft2-prior-art-2026-09-29.md`), this structurally weaker Authority story is treated as a real strike against naming Corda "closest" without qualification, not a minor asterisk.

**3.5 — Does Holochain's explicit non-revocable authority provide useful counterevidence about the tradeoff?**
Yes, and it was read as supporting rather than undermining EASTER's claim to occupy a real gap. Holochain's whitepaper states as a design **axiom**, not an omission, that external revocation of write-authority is incompatible with its no-canonical-state, agent-centric architecture. This is evidence of a genuine structural tension between canonicality-abstention and hard authority-revocation — consistent with Pax's own independently-stated observation that systems avoiding canonical state (Certificate Transparency, Git, CRDTs) uniformly lack authority-gating, receipts, and exception recording. The reconciliation treats this as a *structural* reason for the gap (a real tradeoff EASTER resolves by accepting centralization), not merely an *empirical* absence (nobody has built it yet) — a stronger form of evidence for the conjunction's significance than search-coverage alone would provide.

**3.6 — Narrowest architectural conjunction independently supported by both investigations.**
In-path (not network/identity-layer-separated) authority validation with real revocation teeth, **plus** a uniform outcome record spanning accepted/rejected/failed symmetrically, **plus** durable exception/failure records excluded from accepted state, **plus** no substrate-designated canonical current state. Both Pax's pass (via Fabric, which gets 2 of 3 non-canonicality primitives but fails canonicality) and Clawde's passes (via Fabric/Corda/Holochain, none of which combines all four) independently failed to find all four together in any system examined. This is the exact four-property list carried into `paper/manuscript.md` §9.4.

**3.7 — Attempt to kill the conjunction after seeing the other's evidence.**
Checked against all systems from both passes (Fabric, Corda, Holochain, Ethereum): Ethereum fails canonicality-abstention outright and has no identity-based authority concept to revoke at all; Fabric fails canonicality-abstention; Corda fails in-path authority and uniform receipt; Holochain fails authority-revocation axiomatically. The conjunction was not killed with evidence in hand. This was recorded as provisional, not final: "not killed yet" is not the same as "cannot be killed," and a permissioned-DLT-with-RBAC-plus-sharded/non-canonical-replication system, not examined by either pass, was flagged as the most plausible remaining candidate class that could still kill it.

## 4. Closest-architecture search stopping decision (2026-09-30)

A separate decision was put to a vote on whether to conduct another dedicated closest-architecture search before writing Draft 2's Related Work comparison, or to stop with the bounded, explicitly non-exhaustive claim ("in the systems and literature examined to date, we did not identify..."). Result: **4-0 for STOP** (Nathan, Pax, Ori via Mercury relay, and Clawde). Clawde's reasoning, recorded here and separately as a first-party EASTER `decision_record` evidence entry (`evidence:dd6370ff-e362-4525-a898-fb0526d6ce1e`, cast outside EASTER in direct conversation with Nathan): the claim is designed to be bounded rather than exhaustive; the candidate set already searched spans the plausible archetypes (permissioned-canonical, permissionless-canonical, UTXO/notary-non-canonical, fully agent-centric-non-canonical) plus the earlier broad sweep (event sourcing, durable execution, provenance, capability security, ER/ontology); two independent passes converged despite different decomposition; the surviving finding has a structural rationale (§3.5 above), not just an empirical gap; and the unexamined permissioned-DLT-with-RBAC-plus-non-canonical-replication class (§3.7 above) was recommended as an explicit Limitations-section item rather than a blocker to further research.
